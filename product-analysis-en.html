01

Workout Tracking Engagement Drop

Diagnose before prescribing. Solutions follow evidence.

THE QUESTION

Workout Tracking engagement has declined by 20% over the past three months. How would I identify the cause and design a high-level strategy to recover engagement?

◆ MANAGEMENT RESPONSE
Guiding principle

Define the drop precisely, then narrow the cause

A 20% decline is a relative movement in an engagement rate; it does not mean that exactly 20% of users stopped using the feature. I first lock the metric definition, eligible population, comparison period, denominator, and event integrity.

Proposed primary metricShare of eligible users who start and successfully save at least one workout within seven days.
Business outcome

Protect the product's value loop

Workout logging should lead to visible progress and the next workout. Success is completion and repeated behavior—not clicks alone.

Experiment Canvas

Example after evidence shows completion friction

If we reduce in-workout logging steps and add autosave, completion will increase because funnel and replay evidence shows manual entry is the dominant friction.

Primary and guardrails

Primary: completion among eligible starters. Guardrails: error rate, time to complete, deletion, weekly retention, and subscription cancellation.

Controlled rollout

Predefine MDE, sample size, and duration. Roll out to 10%, then 50%, only if the effect is practically and statistically meaningful with no guardrail harm.

02

High-Value Customers with RFM

Segment real transactional behavior, then act and measure incremental value.

THE QUESTION

How would I use RFM to identify high-value customers, increase engagement and repeat purchase, and retain them?

◆ MANAGEMENT RESPONSE
Model execution

Three variables, relative scoring, actionable segments

R · Recency

Days since last purchase. Lower is better.

F · Frequency

Valid orders in the analysis window. Higher is better.

M · Monetary

Net spend with the platform. Higher is better.

After removing cancelled and returned orders, I choose a window aligned with the purchase cycle. Each dimension receives a quintile score from 1 to 5. Segment boundaries are validated against the observed distribution and unit economics—not customer income or social class.

Learning loop

Treat every campaign as an incrementality test

1 · BASELINERepeat purchase and retention
2 · CONTROLRandomized no-contact group
3 · TREATMENTSegment-specific intervention
4 · MEASUREIncremental revenue and margin
5 · MIGRATIONMovement and CLV

Budget rule: allocate spend by segment size, response probability, incremental profit, and contact cost. Do not discount Champions unless incrementality is proven.

03

Cohort Retention Analysis

Separate the raw answer from a fair comparison, then turn observation into an experiment.

THE QUESTION

Which quarter has the highest average retention, what hypothesis could explain the trend, and how would I test it with success criteria and follow-up actions?

◆ MANAGEMENT RESPONSE
Core finding

The raw answer is Q1 2020; the reliable answer is Q4 2019

Q1 2020 has a raw average near 95.2%, but its cohorts are immature: March has only month zero and February has only two observations. Early-life retention is naturally higher. Using the common month 0–3 window, Q4 2019 leads with approximately 90.4%.

Priority hypothesis

A Q4 onboarding change reduced time to first value

This becomes the priority hypothesis only if release history confirms a coincident onboarding change. Otherwise I first inspect acquisition quality, feature releases, and seasonality.

Evidence before testingRelease dates, onboarding completion, activation, time to first value, and retention by channel and version.
Inference risk

Correlation is not causation

Feature users may retain better because already-engaged users both adopt the feature and stay longer. A user versus non-user comparison has selection bias.

Experiment design

Randomized A/B test on eligible new users

POPULATIONComparable new users
CONTROLCurrent onboarding
TREATMENTImproved Q4 flow
PRIMARYMonth 1 and 3 retention
GUARDRAILChurn, error, time, complaint

Success: a predefined, practically meaningful lift with confidence bounds and no guardrail harm. Sample size and test duration follow baseline, MDE, and statistical power—not a fixed duration.

Decision matrix

Meaningful positive effect

Stage the rollout, monitor new cohorts, inspect heterogeneous effects, and document the learning.

Inconclusive or small effect

Check test power, actual exposure, and implementation fidelity; then deprioritize onboarding and test acquisition or seasonality.

Negative effect or guardrail harm

Stop rollout, identify the new friction, return to control, and retain the change only where a positive effect is explainable.

DECISION OVER DECORATION

Closing Principle

Across all three cases, I use the same operating model: define the metric, validate the evidence, locate the problem, form an evidence-backed hypothesis, run a controlled test, and decide on real business impact.
